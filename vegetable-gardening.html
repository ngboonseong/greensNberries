Getting Started

Vegetable gardening is one of the most rewarding ways to grow your own food. Whether you have a backyard plot, raised beds, or just a few containers on a balcony, you can harvest fresh, nutritious vegetables from your own space.

Planning Your Garden

  • Location — Most vegetables need 6–8 hours of direct sunlight daily
  • Soil — Rich, well-drained soil amended with compost is key. Test your soil pH (aim for 6.0–7.0)
  • Size — Start small. A 4x8 ft raised bed or a few large pots is plenty for beginners
  • Season — Know your hardiness zone and plant according to your local frost dates

What To Plant

Choose vegetables that suit your climate and taste. Easy wins for beginners:

  • Warm season — Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, beans, squash, corn
  • Cool season — Lettuce, spinach, carrots, peas, broccoli, kale, radishes
  • Perennials — Asparagus, rhubarb, artichokes (come back year after year)

Tip: Grow what you love to eat. There's nothing like the taste of a sun-warmed tomato you grew yourself!

Seasonal Care

  • Watering — Water deeply and consistently, preferably at the base to avoid foliar diseases
  • Mulching — Apply 2–3 inches of organic mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds
  • Feeding — Side-dress with compost or a balanced organic fertilizer mid-season
  • Pest control — Encourage beneficial insects, use row covers, and rotate crops yearly

Harvesting

Harvest in the early morning when vegetables are crisp and full of moisture. Pick regularly to encourage continued production — especially for beans, zucchini, and tomatoes. Most vegetables taste best when harvested at their peak ripeness.
